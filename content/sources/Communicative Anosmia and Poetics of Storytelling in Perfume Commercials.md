**Summary**
Leleń argues that the defining structural problem of perfume advertising is "communicative anosmia": the viewer is by definition deprived of the sense being advertised. To compensate, perfume commercials deploy palimpsestic narrative strategies — layering fairy tales, myths, literary references, and cinematic intertexts — to achieve intersubjective immersion. Ads do not describe the scent; they build fictional worlds dense enough that the viewer incorporates the product as part of their own identity narrative. The theoretical framework draws on narratology, literary studies, semiotics, and film studies rather than the advertising or sociology approaches more common in the field.

**Key points**
- Communicative anosmia: the viewer of any perfume commercial is structurally deprived of the product's primary quality
- Compensation strategy: narrative perspectivization — prompting the viewer to adopt spatial, cognitive, emotional, and sensual positions of characters
- Palimpsestic narrative: ads layer multiple source traditions (fairy tale, myth, novel, film genre) simultaneously, not one at a time
- Synesthetic legacy: Baudelaire's "Correspondences" establishes the template of cross-sensory perfume evocation used in contemporary ads
- Rihanna Reb'l Fleur (2011): palindromic structure playable forwards and backwards; Jekyll/Hyde duality; Alice Through the Looking-Glass intertextuality; slogan flips ("Bad Feels so Good" / "Good Feels so Bad")
- Chanel No 5 (2004, Luhrmann/Kidman, $33M): intertextual with Moulin Rouge! and Eyes Wide Shut; "vampire effect" risk from celebrity association
- Chanel No 5 (2014, Bündchen): surfer body in water as visual surrogate for fragrance diffusion; Great Gatsby bridge allusion marks character liminality
- Kenzo World (2016, Spike Jonze/Qualley): subverts erotic body convention via convulsive non-feminine dance; Celtic/chivalric allusions; inability to conform recast as power
- Miss Dior (2019, Portman): quotes Gertrude Stein's "Rose is a rose"; perfume bottle and female figure made visually identical through circular montage
- Bodies in constant movement in ads enact the diffusive property of fragrance

**Claude's notes**
The literary-studies methodology is unusual in perfume commercial analysis and yields genuinely original observations: the palindromic Reb'l Fleur structure, the Stein quotation as poetic surrogate for the scent itself, the identification of Portman's body with the bottle. "Communicative anosmia" is the most portable concept in the article — it explains *why* perfume ads have converged on this set of strategies (structural necessity) rather than just describing *what* they do. Published in *Media Biznes Kultura* (University of the National Education Commission, Kraków) with a DOI; appears to be a genuine peer-reviewed journal in media and communication studies.
