## What it is

Mainstream Western perfume commercials cast white protagonists at rates far exceeding any plausible market rationale. Deng & Shi found 95% white protagonists across 45 major-brand ads; non-white actors appeared in only two cases (CK One and Versace Man Eau Fraiche). Unlike many product categories, perfume ads are also "less localized" — they do not substitute local models for target markets. The same white protagonists appear globally.

Whiteness is not incidental. It is part of the aspirational package: the gender myth being sold is specifically the ideal of Western heteronormative bourgeois white culture. The "conservative Englishness look" (Stuart Hall, quoted in Deng & Shi) is a deliberate production choice.

## Why it matters

Racial casting in perfume ads is not a side issue to gender analysis — it is constitutive of the gender myth. The femininity and masculinity being sold are specifically white bourgeois variants. The 95% figure makes visible how narrow the ideal is and how systematically it is reproduced across brands, even "inclusive" ones.

## Key points

- 43 of 45 ads sampled by Deng & Shi used white protagonists^[#source/peer-reviewed] — though note the sample is non-random and based on search engine results
- Non-white appearances were confined to specific brand extensions positioned as "inclusive" exceptions (CK One, Versace Man Eau Fraiche)
- Hall on the Chanel casting process: achieving the "conservative Englishness look" required choosing models with "pale white skin, with lighter hair and softer features"
- Perfume ads are less localized than other ad categories: white models are used globally, regardless of target market demographics
- The aspirational world constructed in the ads is geographically European (castles, metropolitan cityscapes) as well as racially white
- Tsutsumi: NCT DOJAEJUNG's "Perfume" MV breaks the white male protagonist norm with three Korean leads — but the only female figure in the video, the recipient of the perfumes the members design, is white^[#source/secondary]
- This partial subversion is legible as a negotiation: Asian male protagonists distinguish the product as Korean-market, while the white female recipient preserves a Western aspirational coding

## Evidence across sources

Deng & Shi establish the statistical pattern; Tsutsumi provides a case that partially subverts it while inadvertently confirming its structural grip. Both sources treat whiteness as tied to class aspiration and bourgeois Western ideals, not as a freestanding racial preference.
