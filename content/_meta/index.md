## Research threads

### Thread 1: The Structural Impossibility of Scent Advertising
Concepts: [[Communicative Anosmia]]
Sources: [[Communicative Anosmia and Poetics of Storytelling in Perfume Commercials]], [[The False Obvious Gender Myth in Perfume Commercials]]
The genre's defining formal and ideological problem. Both sources converge on the idea that because scent cannot be transmitted, the ad substitutes narrative — but they disagree on whether that narrative is primarily craft or primarily ideology.

### Thread 2: Gender and Representation in Fragrance Marketing
Concepts: [[Gender Myth in Perfume Advertising]]
Sources: [[The False Obvious Gender Myth in Perfume Commercials]], [[Communicative Anosmia and Poetics of Storytelling in Perfume Commercials]]
The genre's representational politics. Contested on the Kenzo World problem: does formal subversion of erotic convention constitute ideological escape?

### Thread 3: Dior as Case Study (research thread, not yet compiled)
Both sources analyze the same Dior commercials (Miss Dior/Portman; J'adore/Theron) from opposed methodological positions — Leleń on narrative craft and literary allusion, Deng & Shi on ideology and symptomatic reading. A compare-and-contrast essay structure is built into the vault.

## Open questions

- Are there sources on perfume advertising outside the Western/Eurocentric frame? Both current sources assume a Western industry.
- What's the relationship between communicative anosmia and other "impossible media" problems (e.g., advertising taste, texture)?
- Is Kenzo World (Jonze/Qualley) a genuine counter-example to the gender myth thesis, or does it remain captured by it?
- Does gender-neutral fragrance marketing (CK One and successors) change the representational logic, or reproduce it?
- How does the predominantly female consumer base of perfume interact with Mulvey's assumption of a male spectating subject?
