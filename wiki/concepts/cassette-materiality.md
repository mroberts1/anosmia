# Cassette Materiality

**Summary**: The physical, cultural, and economic properties of the cassette tape that explain its persistence in DIY music scenes — including its hybridity, re-recordability, low cost, creative packaging possibilities, and role as connective tissue across local, translocal, and virtual music scenes.

**Sources**: `Duester Bennett How does materiality bite back.pdf`, `duester-2023-there-s-a-lot-of-freedom...pdf`, `Duester Cassette Comeback.pdf`, `978-3-030-44659-8_10.pdf`

**Last updated**: 2026-04-25

---

## The cassette as hybrid format

The cassette is a hybrid format — not in the sense of being "readopted" as a digitally contextualized object, but because it has always accommodated multiple uses simultaneously: home recording, distribution, gifting, trading, creative packaging. Download codes added to contemporary cassette releases are one more layer of hybridity, not a transformation. (source: `Duester Bennett How does materiality bite back.pdf`)

Material properties enabling hybridity:
- **Re-recordability** — blank tapes can be reused; thrift-store tapes can be recycled
- **Small and sturdy** — cheap to ship; robust enough for postal mail
- **A/B side structure** — creates natural conceptual division for split releases and albums
- **Extended runtime** — up to 45+ minutes per side (vs. ~4–6 min for a 7-inch at 45rpm)
- **No minimum quantities** — cassette duplication can be done in runs as small as 1

(source: `duester-2023-there-s-a-lot-of-freedom...pdf`)

## Why cassettes persist: the material argument

Duester and Bennett argue that Magaudda's "crisis of authenticity" explanation (digital music feels inauthentic → vinyl reappropriated as antidote) captures only part of the picture. Cassettes persist because:

1. **Genre history** — ambient, noise, punk, experimental music were built on cassettes; using the format means participating in that lineage. Mike Nigro (Oxtail Recordings): "When you release that kind of music on that format you are participating in that history."
2. **Cost** — cassettes are cheaper than vinyl for small DIY runs; no pressing plant minimums
3. **Community** — cassette releases function as calling cards, relationship tools, and scene participation tokens
4. **Tactile contrast** — the physical ritual of playing a cassette (inserting, flipping, rewinding) contrasts with infinite digital streaming as a deliberate, slowing experience

(source: `Duester Bennett How does materiality bite back.pdf`)

## Circuit of practice

Duester and Bennett extend Magaudda's "circuit of practice" model (objects ↔ meanings ↔ doings) to cover production and distribution as well as consumption, and embed it in Peterson and Bennett's three-tier scene model:

- **Local scene**: face-to-face transactions, record store relationships, concert distribution
- **Translocal scene**: DIY tours, mail-order, compilation cassettes introducing local artists to distant scenes
- **Virtual scene**: Bandcamp pages, social media, podcasts like Tabs Out

Cassette tapes circulate across all three levels simultaneously. Oxtail Recordings (Sydney/Delaware) flows outward: local → translocal → virtual. Muzan Editions (Osaka) flows inward: virtual → translocal → attempts at local. (source: `Duester Bennett How does materiality bite back.pdf`)

## Material limitation as creative resource

The cassette's constraints — fixed A/B sides, finite runtime, physical production lead time — are not bugs but features in DIY collaboration. They provide a ready-made framework for split releases and collaborative projects that digital distribution does not offer. Fleisher (2015): "postdigital sensibility" — deliberate engagement with analog materiality as contrast to digital overabundance. (source: `duester-2023-there-s-a-lot-of-freedom...pdf`)

Digital releases require artists to negotiate length, track count, and format from scratch. A split cassette imposes those parameters immediately, enabling creative focus. Scott McLatchie (Melbourne): "With records it kind of has to be this way. But with tapes, we can add this and take away this and do all that stuff."

## Production infrastructure

Current cassette production is concentrated in two companies:
- **NAC** (National Audio Company, Springfield, Missouri) — survived the 2000s trough via religious ministry orders; introduced Super Ferric tape (2019) after its last magnetic tape supplier ceased production
- **Duplication.ca** (Canada) — primary source for many international labels; offers 50+ color options

**Mulann** (France) produces Fox audio tape for the European market. Japanese domestic manufacturers have largely closed; Japanese DIY labels now import. (source: `Duester Cassette Comeback.pdf`)

## The "cassette scene" problem

Duester and Bennett caution against flattening all cassette users into a "cassette scene." Artists, labels, and listeners using cassettes belong to pre-existing scenes (ambient, punk, noise, etc.) with their own values and geographies. The cassette is a format that circulates within those scenes, not a scene-forming object in itself. Muzan Editions is a Japanese label in cassette terms but finds almost no audience within Japan — 98% of customers are abroad. (source: `Duester Bennett How does materiality bite back.pdf`)
