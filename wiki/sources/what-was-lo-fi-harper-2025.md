# What was Lo-Fi? — Adam Harper (2025)

**Summary**: A review essay historicizing the term "lo-fi" as it was used in independent music from the 1980s–1990s, arguing it was a "historicized position" that bundled together poor sound quality, home-recording, DIY, and amateurism — things that had no necessary relationship — and contrasting that history with today's lofi hip hop.

## Core arguments

### 1. Lo-fi as a historicized position

Harper uses Allan F. Moore's concept of the "historicized position" to argue that lo-fi was never an objective property of recordings. Rather, it was a cultural construction — "ascribed, not inscribed" — assembled through perception and discourse. The elements conflated under "lo-fi" (poor sound quality, home-taping, DIY production, amateurism, authenticity) had no necessary relationship to one another, even though 1990s indie rock treated them as interchangeable.

Alexandra Supper is quoted: "a recording cannot be considered 'hi-fi' or 'lo-fi' in and of itself... it is the contexts surrounding the recording and the conditions under which it is listened to that make it one or the other." (source: `What was Lo-Fi .pdf`)

### 2. The lo-fi narrative

The "lo-fi narrative" is Harper's term for the conflation described above: music recorded at home on tape entails poor sound quality, but is nevertheless worthwhile or even preferable. This narrative consolidated around indie rock by the mid-1990s, when "lo-fi" became a noun and category (not just a descriptor). See [[lo-fi]] for full treatment.

### 3. Lo-fi as noisy channel

Harper's aesthetic theory: recording imperfections function as *noise* in a *signal/noise channel*, where "the music" is the signal. Appreciating lo-fi means engaging with that tension — sometimes enjoying the hiss, sometimes listening despite it, sometimes experiencing dramatic conflict between signal and noise. He calls the most intense cases "dramas of the imperfect signal." See [[noisy-channel]].

### 4. The break with lofi hip hop

The 2017+ "lofi hip hop" phenomenon (Lofi Girl YouTube channel, "beats to relax/study to") shares the word but is fundamentally different: relaxing, mild analog texture added digitally, no drama of imperfection, no DIY/anti-industry politics. Harper argues it has "stretched the association of the term 'lo-fi' with recording imperfections... perhaps to the point of breaking." See [[lofi-hip-hop]].

## Structure of the paper

1. **Introduction** — contrasts 1988 WFMU "Lo-Fi" concert flyer with 2020 *Lofi Ping Pong* Nintendo game
2. **Lo-fi as historicized position** — the lo-fi narrative, perception, and discourse
3. **The term "lo-fi" as historicized position** — etymology from "low fidelity" (post-1950s hi-fi), Berger's WFMU segment (1986), 1994 NYT / *Option* "Lo-Fi Lovefest"
4. **Sound quality as historicized position** — Robert Johnson example; analog noise as archaic; technostalgia, hauntology
5. **Cassette culture as historicized position** — *OP*, *Sound Choice*, *Option* magazines; cassette culture ≠ lo-fi
6. **Amateurism, primitivism, and slackers** — Jandek, Daniel Johnston, Beat Happening; the slacker figure of the early 1990s
7. **Lo-fi as noisy channel** — signal/noise theory, recording imperfections, authenticity paradox
8. **Dramas of the imperfect signal** — Daniel Johnston, Pavement, bootlegs, folk revival
9. **What will lo-fi be?** — lofi hip hop, the term's migration
