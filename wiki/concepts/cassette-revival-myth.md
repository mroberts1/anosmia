# Cassette Revival Myth

**Summary**: The mainstream "cassette comeback" narrative — recycled by news outlets since the late 2000s — is reductive and misleading: cassettes never completely disappeared, commercial sales figures miss the DIY scene entirely, and the format's persistence reflects material and cultural factors far more complex than nostalgia.

**Sources**: `Duester Cassette Comeback.pdf`, `978-3-030-44659-8_10.pdf`

**Last updated**: 2026-04-25

---

## The narrative

Since around 2007–2010, news outlets have periodically declared a "cassette comeback" or "cassette revival," typically triggered by annual sales figures showing increased cassette sales in the UK and US. Articles cite:
- UK: 2.1 million cassettes sold in 2015; +112% in 2016
- US: 129,000 (2016) → 219,000 (2018) units per Nielsen
- Signature releases: Guardians of the Galaxy soundtracks (best-performing US cassette title in 2017), Metallica demo reissue (*No Life 'Til Leather*, ~3,000 copies at Record Store Day 2015)

These accounts frame cassettes as nostalgic gimmicks embraced by urban hipsters or as an ironic counterculture trend. Duester argues this narrative has "not changed in the past 15 years" while being continuously recycled. (source: `Duester Cassette Comeback.pdf`)

---

## Why the narrative is wrong

### 1. Cassettes never disappeared
DIY music scenes used cassettes continuously throughout the 2000s. In the same year (2007) that some outlets declared the "death of the cassette," others noted their continued use by DIY devotees. Chicago's Lillerne Tape Club was founded in 2007 — cassette culture's supposed "fatal year." Big Love Records in Tokyo displays a hand-painted shelf sign: "TAPES Never Went Away." (source: `Duester Cassette Comeback.pdf`)

### 2. Sales statistics miss DIY entirely
Nielsen and industry association figures (RIAA, ARIA) track commercial sales — major label merchandise — and often group cassettes under "other" formats. They do not capture:
- Bandcamp releases (~500,000 cassettes sold in 2021)
- Discogs marketplace (~286,000 cassettes sold in 2021)
- Self-released, barcode-free DIY tapes sold at shows or through direct mail

Discogs data shows ~2,500 new cassette titles in punk alone in 2017. At a conservative average of 50 copies each, that's 125,000 cassettes in one genre from one platform — dwarfing most individual "comeback" titles cited by journalists. (source: `Duester Cassette Comeback.pdf`)

### 3. The "comeback" releases often weren't played
Are cassette sales evidence of a format revival? Many high-profile "comeback" cassettes were likely bought as merchandise or novelty items rather than played — especially as working cassette players became scarce. The Guardians of the Galaxy or Metallica purchases indicate fandom, not rekindled interest in cassette audio. (source: `Duester Cassette Comeback.pdf`)

### 4. Vinyl comparison is misleading
"Are cassettes the new vinyl?" (common headline) is a false comparison: vinyl retained steady demand through DJ culture, which maintained turntable infrastructure. Cassette deck infrastructure collapsed and has not recovered. (source: `Duester Cassette Comeback.pdf`)

## What actually drove cassette persistence

1. **Religious ministries** — Joyce Meyer Ministries ordered 250,000 blank tapes per week through the 2000s; similar patterns in Egypt, Yemen, Iran
2. **Cars** — cassette decks standard in vehicles until the late 2000s
3. **DIY scene continuity** — punk, noise, metal, ambient, experimental scenes never stopped; cassettes are historically baked into those genres' identities
4. **Vinyl price increases** — pressing costs rose ~20% (2015–2018), making cassettes relatively more attractive

(source: `Duester Cassette Comeback.pdf`)

---

## Japan as counterexample

Japan's mainstream media framed increased cassette activity in the 2010s as a revival. But domestic cassette production declined continuously since 1988. The apparent "increase" in DIY cassette releases was based on imports from North American manufacturers, not domestic production growth. The enka industry's use of cassettes — the only sector sustaining Japanese domestic production — has itself collapsed by 2023 (only 3 new singles). (source: `978-3-030-44659-8_10.pdf`, `Duester Cassette Comeback.pdf`)

---

## The hardware problem

A genuine cassette revival would require playback infrastructure. The used high-quality deck market has dried up since the mid-2010s. New tape decks: only TASCAM 202MKVII ($499) and TEAC equivalent ($390), both with inferior plastic construction. Cheap "cassette converter" USB devices are sonically inadequate. "As Cassettes Come Back, the Dilemma Is Finding a Tape Deck" (*New York Times*, 2024). (source: `Duester Cassette Comeback.pdf`)
