**Summary**
Student research paper submitted to Professor Roberts for Cinema After Cinema, 1 May 2026. Tsutsumi analyzes how NCT DOJAEJUNG's 2023 mini-album *Perfume* adapts the visual and narrative conventions of Western perfume commercials to the K-Pop industry. The paper traces two main axes of influence: (1) aspirational lifestyle imagery (luxury and nature themes) adapted with an Asian lens; (2) gender conventions systematically subverted — the "flower boy" aesthetic and parasocial fan-idol dynamics replace the muscular heterosexual male protagonist of cologne advertising. The paper draws on Deng & Shi (2024) and Leleń (2022) alongside K-Pop industry sources.

**Key points**
- Western perfume commercials fall into two aspirational categories: luxury (castles, pools, metropolitan glamour) and nature (floral, elemental, "fresh")
- NCT DOJAEJUNG's "Perfume" MV leans toward the nature category but was filmed in Thailand, not Europe — a deliberate Asian repositioning
- 95% white protagonist norm in Western perfume ads (Deng & Shi): "Perfume" breaks this with Asian male leads, but notably includes a white female model as the perfume's recipient
- Male cologne conventions (muscular body, leather jacket, sexual dominance) are entirely absent; "flower boy" aesthetic substitutes: jewelry, silk, floral imagery, emotional vulnerability
- K-Pop's primary audience is women; male idol gender performance is calibrated to female taste, not male aspiration
- Celebrity in perfume ads (Leleń: "polyphonic intertextual associations"): K-Pop extends this via parasocial relationships — fans already feel they "know" the members as individuals
- Each member designed a personal fragrance; blotter papers shipped with album — tactile/olfactory engagement extending beyond visual
- Lyrics deploy scent-as-memory structure (Proust Phenomenon): pulse points, wrist and collar, fragrance as mnemonic for the singer
- "Strawberry Sunday" links taste memory (gourmand fragrance trend) to emotional intimacy
- NCT's "cultural technology" framework (SM Entertainment founder Lee Soo-man): systematic adaptation of global aesthetic conventions to Korean cultural output
