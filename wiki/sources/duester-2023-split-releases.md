# 'There's a Lot of Freedom You Can Have With That Kind of Thing': Vinyl and Cassette Split Releases in the Digital Age — Duester (2023)

**Summary**: Examines "split releases" — recordings split between two artists on a single cassette or vinyl — as a foundational DIY music format that uses the material limitations of analog formats to enable creative collaboration, cost-sharing, and cross-scene networking that digital distribution cannot replicate.

## What is a split release?

A split release features two different artists, one per side of a cassette, 7-inch single, or other physical format with distinct A and B sides. The title format is "Artist A/Artist B – Split." Three-way and four-way splits are common ("X-Way Split"). CDs are used but their continuous playback makes the conceptual split less apparent. (source: `duester-2023-there-s-a-lot-of-freedom...pdf`)

### Scale
- Discogs lists 13,418 cassette splits, 27,037 vinyl splits (11,659 LPs, 11,547 7-inches), 15,947 CD splits
- Punk leads by genre (12,130 releases), followed by Noise (10,455) and Hardcore (9,861)
- Cassette splits surged from 1,895 (2000–2010) to 6,145 (2010–2020) — tripling during the period cassettes were widely considered dead
- ~3,000 new splits released per year in the 2020s

(source: `duester-2023-there-s-a-lot-of-freedom...pdf`)

### Historical origins
- Earliest conceptual predecessor: Al Jolson / Anna Chandler split shellac (1917, Columbia)
- First DIY-format split: X-Pulsion/Streets 7-inch (Belgium, 1978)
- US blueprint: Dischord Records' Faith/Void split (1982, hardcore)
- Extreme: Slap A Ham's *Bllleeeeaaauuurrrrgghhh!* — 73 bands, 84 songs, one 7-inch, 14 minutes (1998)
- Most prolific: Merzbow, engaged with splits "over the past 40 years"

## Why splits persist in the digital age

### Cost-sharing
Vinyl 7-inch production: $621–$1,087 for 50–250 units (Mobineko, Solid Merch, Musicol quotes). Cassette production: $186–$260 for 50 units. Splitting production costs halves (or quarters) the financial risk. Artists with remaining stock risk are particularly motivated to split runs. (source: `duester-2023-there-s-a-lot-of-freedom...pdf`)

### Networking and exposure
A split introduces each artist to the other's fanbase. Boris/Lisa Bella Donna 4-way split (Japan tour, 2019) was explicitly used to lift Bella Donna's exposure among Boris fans. Splits also function as cross-scene connectors: Twaague (Berlin) organized a split between scene companions with different musical backgrounds who would not otherwise have collaborated. (source: `duester-2023-there-s-a-lot-of-freedom...pdf`)

### Material limitation as creative resource
Digital music production is virtually unlimited — any length, any track count, instantly distributable worldwide. Cassette and vinyl impose real constraints (A/B sides, runtime limits, physical production lead times) that serve as a creative *framework* for collaboration. Stephen Hoffman (Tar Pit, Portland): "With cassettes, you're not putting up all this money, so I can fuck around a little bit. It's not gonna be a huge financial burden." (source: `duester-2023-there-s-a-lot-of-freedom...pdf`)

### Postdigital sensibility
Splits represent what Fleisher (2015) calls "postdigital sensibility" — a deliberate engagement with analog materiality as contrast to digital overabundance. The ritual of flipping a cassette or record, the A/B structure, the fixed runtime: these "drawbacks" are part of the attraction. Bandcamp's interface mirrors this by listing tracks in a finite sequence rather than algorithmically flowing into related content. (source: `duester-2023-there-s-a-lot-of-freedom...pdf`)

## Cassette vs. vinyl splits

| Dimension | Cassette split | Vinyl 7-inch split |
|-----------|---------------|-------------------|
| Cost | $186–$260 / 50 units | $621–$1,087 / 50–250 units |
| Minimum quantity | Usually none | Often 100–250 |
| Runtime per side | Up to 45 min+ | ~4–6 min at 45rpm |
| Cultural prestige | "Carefree," DIY | "Legitimate," serious |
| Creative flexibility | High | Lower (must be conceptually tight) |

(source: `duester-2023-there-s-a-lot-of-freedom...pdf`)

Scott McLatchie (Melbourne): "If it's on record it's kind of like: 'this is legitimate!' But with tapes, if you need more, you just make more, you don't need to go through a pressing plant."

---

## Digital integration

Splits don't oppose digital distribution — they complement it. Bandcamp pages for splits cross-reference each artist's page, encouraging discovery. Sean McGee (Tokyo): "I listen to Spotify all day every day. But when it comes to physical media, I'll take either one. I wanna put it in, I wanna listen to it, I wanna open the book." (source: `duester-2023-there-s-a-lot-of-freedom...pdf`)
