# Split Releases

**Summary**: A foundational DIY music format — two artists sharing one cassette or vinyl record, one per side — that uses physical material constraints to enable cost-sharing, cross-scene networking, and creative collaboration that digital distribution cannot replicate.

**Sources**: `duester-2023-there-s-a-lot-of-freedom...pdf`

**Last updated**: 2026-04-25

---

## What is a split release?

A split release features two different artists, each contributing recordings to one side (A or B) of a physical format with distinct sides — typically a cassette tape or 7-inch vinyl single. The standard title format is "Artist A / Artist B – Split." Three-way and four-way ("X-Way Split") configurations are common. CDs are used but their continuous playback makes the conceptual split less apparent. (source: `duester-2023-there-s-a-lot-of-freedom...pdf`)

---

## History

- **1917**: First conceptual predecessor — Al Jolson / Anna Chandler split shellac (Columbia)
- **1978**: First DIY-format split — X-Pulsion/Streets 7-inch (Belgium, punk)
- **1979**: UK splits Restricted Hours/The Syndicate and Pneumania/UK Decay
- **1982**: US blueprint — Dischord Records' Faith/Void hardcore split
- **1998**: Conceptual extreme — Slap A Ham's *Bllleeeeaaauuurrrrgghhh!*: 73 bands, 84 songs, one 7-inch, 14 minutes

Merzbow (Japanese noise) has produced splits "over the past 40 years" — among the most prolific split artists. (source: `duester-2023-there-s-a-lot-of-freedom...pdf`)

## Scale

From Discogs data:
- **13,418** cassette splits listed
- **27,037** vinyl splits (11,659 LPs; 11,547 7-inches)
- **15,947** CD splits (peaked in 2000s US punk; now declining)
- Punk leads by genre: 12,130 releases; Noise: 10,455; Hardcore: 9,861
- USA leads by country: 24,518 entries; UK: 6,340
- Cassette splits nearly tripled from ~1,900 (2000–2010) to ~6,145 (2010–2020)
- ~3,000 new splits released per year in the 2020s

(source: `duester-2023-there-s-a-lot-of-freedom...pdf`)

## Why splits matter

### Cost-sharing
Vinyl production is expensive and requires minimum quantities. Splitting costs between two artists (and sometimes multiple labels) makes physical releasing viable for grassroots projects. Jonathan Borges (Monorail Trespassing, LA): "a lot of people... splitting a release between five or six different labels to break down the cost to a tiny amount." (source: `duester-2023-there-s-a-lot-of-freedom...pdf`)

### Exposure and networking
A split introduces each artist to the other's fanbase. Established acts use splits to lift lesser-known artists. The Boris/Lisa Bella Donna 4-way split (Japan tour, 2019) was explicitly used to expose Bella Donna to Boris fans. In the pre-internet era, splits were the primary method for building relationships across long distances: "there were so many split cassettes from people on different ends of the world" (Borges). (source: `duester-2023-there-s-a-lot-of-freedom...pdf`)

### Creative framework
Digital music production is effectively unlimited in length and structure. The cassette or vinyl's fixed A/B sides and runtime impose a creative constraint that both simplifies and focuses collaboration. Stephen Hoffman (Tar Pit, Portland): "With cassettes I'm not putting up all this money, so I can fuck around a little bit." Artists can include covers, one-offs, or experimental pieces alongside their main recordings. (source: `duester-2023-there-s-a-lot-of-freedom...pdf`)

## Cassette vs. vinyl splits

| Dimension | Cassette split | Vinyl 7-inch split |
|-----------|---------------|-------------------|
| Cost (50 units) | ~$186–$260 | ~$621–$1,087 |
| Minimum quantity | Usually none | Often 100–250 |
| Runtime per side | Up to 45 min+ | ~4–6 min at 45rpm |
| Cultural prestige | "Carefree," DIY | "Legitimate," serious |
| Creative flexibility | High | Lower (must be conceptually tight) |

Scott McLatchie (Melbourne): "If it's on record it's kind of like: 'This is legitimate!' But with tapes, if you need more, you just make more, you don't need to go through a pressing plant." (source: `duester-2023-there-s-a-lot-of-freedom...pdf`)

## Digital integration

Splits don't oppose digital distribution — they complement it. Bandcamp pages for splits cross-reference each artist's profile. The platform's finite track-list structure (unlike Spotify's algorithmic flow) mirrors the physical A/B format. The material limitation of the split becomes a navigational structure online as well. (source: `duester-2023-there-s-a-lot-of-freedom...pdf`)
